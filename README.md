Main chapter

The storm tore across the mining colony’s outer rim, slashing through the steel corridors like a living thing. Dr. Elara Voss stumbled through the flickering light, clutching the memory core that pulsed hot against her chest. Behind her, the echo of mechanized footsteps grew louder—sharp, deliberate, human in rhythm but far too heavy to belong to a man. The comm in her ear hissed with static; someone was still tracking her signal. She bit down a curse and dove into a maintenance hatch, the metallic air thick with ozone and fear.

For a moment she forgot to breathe. The hatch sealed, and the warning prompt unfurled in her retinal display: SIMULATION ENVIRONMENT: GITHUB. Columns of code streamed like rainfall around her, each line alive with branching dangers. Merge conflicts detonated silently in the distance, rippling through the repositories like landmines. Pull requests flickered open and shut—some friendly, others carrying the fingerprints of whoever had already killed her team.

Elara forced herself to focus. The memory core in her hand appeared as a single blinking icon in the air, linked to one repository, one branch: voss/last-run. She zoomed in. The branch lagged behind main; someone else had already pushed new commits. At the top of the list, an open pull request pulsed with a title that made her throat tighten: hotfix: purge survivors.

“Control, this is Voss,” she hissed into her comm. “If you can still hear me: someone’s in the system who knows our protocols. They’re rewriting the logs.” Only static answered her. No voice, no confirmation.

She opened the pull request. The diff was short, clinical: a few lines of code that rerouted the evacuation algorithms toward collapsing shafts. Not sloppy, not impulsive. This was deliberate. Beneath the changes sat a single signature, a single username she recognized from internal audits: root-ghost.

A new notification flashed across her display:
NEW COMMENT ON PR #42 – root-ghost: "Still running, Doctor? You're behind on reviews."

The corridor outside the hatch shuddered again. The sound of metal on metal drew closer, as if the entire colony were scrolling along with the code. Elara clenched her jaw, selected her own branch, and opened a new pull request. Title: override: manual escape route.

If no one else could review her work, she would have to break her own rules.
